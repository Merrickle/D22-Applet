D21 / D22 cheat sheet

Lumber & building materials — quick math for the aisle
Board feet & price

Board feet always use the nominal size (a 2x6 counts as 2×6), not the actual 1½×5½.

Deck boards, joists & screws

Composite decking usually wants 16" OC straight, 12" OC on a 45. Check the board's own instructions.

Wall framing

Openings add roughly 4 pieces each (two kings, two jacks) plus header stock — not counted here.

Concrete: slab, footing & post holes

Bag yields are the printed averages. Ordering ready-mix? Anything over about 1 cu yd is usually cheaper by truck.

Sheet goods: plywood, OSB, drywall
Bulk: mulch, soil, sand & gravel

One cubic yard covers about 108 sq ft at 3" deep, 162 sq ft at 2".

Fence run

Rule of thumb for post depth: a third of the post above grade, below the frost line, whichever is deeper.

Measure converter

Accepts fractions, decimals and feet-inches. Rounds to the nearest 1/16.

Pressure treated: which one

Use categoryWhere it goes
Above groundRails, pickets, decking boards that drain and dry. Not for anything touching soil.
Ground contactPosts, joists and beams near grade, anything on or in soil, or where it can't dry out. Also the safe call for critical framing.
Permanent wood foundationHeavier retention again, for structural foundation use. Special order in most stores.
Cut endsEvery field cut or bore in treated lumber needs an end-cut preservative brushed on, even on ground contact stock.

Fasteners with treated wood

FastenerCall
Hot-dip galvanizedMinimum for exterior treated wood.
StainlessBest life, and the usual pick within a few miles of salt water or with cedar and redwood staining concerns.
Electro-galvanized / brightInterior dry only. It will streak and fail outdoors.
AluminumDon't. Modern copper-based treatments corrode aluminum flashing and fasteners in contact.

Spans: look them up, don't guess Joist, rafter and beam spans change with species, grade, spacing, load and local code, so there's no single number to memorize. Use the span tables on the in-aisle cards, the IRC deck tables (R507) or AWC's DCA6, and send anything unusual to a designer or the permit office.

Customer questions worth asking first Is it touching the ground? Is it structural? Indoors or out? Those three answers pick the treatment, the fastener and the grade most of the time.
